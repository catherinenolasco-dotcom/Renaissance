Case studies

Five projects, with what I would change

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

Ridecell had no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and employees had no tracked channel for HR questions.

What I did

I built a 20+ procedure SOP library, launched ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs, and made Sapling the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

Result

People Operations grew from 3 countries to 5 with 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits. Approval cycles dropped from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day were resolved within 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created audit risk. Early on, some people pushed back that a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

I built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form requiring documentation and approval before any HRIS change, and Notion New Hire and Offboarding workflows, bringing stakeholders in from the start.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed before, and preboarding within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India needed accurate severance and cost numbers, and Payroll and IT needed one place to follow every step.

What I did

I used Claude and Gemini to model severance, COBRA cost and COBRA months owed and to draft agreements, and tracked every step on a shared spreadsheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

Leadership saw RIF costs for the first time, and agreement preparation dropped from 20 minutes each to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

A first plan for Renaissance

A proposed plan built around the responsibilities in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet Legal, Finance, HR colleagues and current vendors to learn how mobility, immigration and relocation work today.
  • Inventory open cases, deadlines, costs, vendors and service levels in one place.
  • Read the existing policies and SOPs and note where they are thin.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Set up one tracker for cases, deadlines and costs, with a clear escalation path for mobility matters.
  • Draft the missing SOPs with named owners and backup coverage.
  • Agree with Legal and Finance on what status, cost and risk reporting should look like.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Roll out the tracker and SOPs with the people who will use them.
  • Hold the first vendor review against service levels, timelines and budget.
  • Send the first status, cost and risk report and collect feedback.
By month 6

The outcome

  • Mobility and immigration run as a documented program with owned vendors and a known escalation point.
  • Managers and employees know where to ask, and policy questions get consistent answers.
  • Leadership has a regular view of cost and risk, and People Relations time goes to the conversations that need a human.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the team, Legal, Finance and the vendors.
